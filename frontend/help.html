How the Part Cost Estimator works

Upload a STEP file, check the inputs, and read the results stage by stage. Every rate and rule comes from Master data.

0

Projects & parts

On Dashboard create a project and a variant, then Import a STEP part or assembly (split automatically) or many parts with the Excel template. Parts keeps every revision and its history.

1

Upload

On Estimate, choose the STEP file (units mm), part type and material, then click Analyse part.

2

Check inputs

Yellow fields are yours to change. Defaults come from master data, and results update as you type.

3

Read results

Grey italic cells are calculated. Bold rows with a dark line above are the totals that matter.

4

Maintain data

Replace example values in Master data with your own rates and catalogues.

Part register

Process suggestion & moulded-in inserts

StepHow the process is suggested (click “why?” to see it for a part)
1 · MaterialIts part type in Master data → Materials sets the family: plastics → moulding, ADC12 / Al alloys → die casting, cast iron → foundry, steel / aluminium sheet grades → sheet metal. An insert material (brass, …) → bought-out
2 · GeometryEach process's rule (Part register setup → Processes) scores the main wall, the share of the surface at that constant wall, bends, fill ratio and size; the highest of the family wins
3 · InsertsMoulded-in inserts (insert_count) make it insert moulding or insert die casting
Insert weightΣ quantity × insert volume (own 3D file, or shape formula × dimensions) × insert density
Total part weightBody weight (body volume without inserts × density) + insert weight

On Import, a smaller part largely enclosed by a bigger one in the same assembly is offered as a moulded-in insert of that body. Confirm it, and it is linked to the body with its quantity instead of becoming a BOM child. On Parts you can link inserts by hand and give each its bought-out price, source and month.

Stage A

Geometry & net weight

ResultHow it is calculated
VolumeExact solid volume from the CAD kernel (OpenCascade)
Net part weightVolume (cm³) × density (g/cm³)
Bounding box L × W × HTight box along the model's X/Y/Z axes, L ≥ W ≥ H
Projected areaShadow of the part along X, Y and Z without through-holes; the largest is the opening direction

Export the part in its moulding / casting orientation. CAD units must be millimetres.

Stage B

Gross weight & RM cost

Part typeGross weightMaterial used per part
PlasticNet × (1 + runner %)Gross × (1 + purge / start-up loss %)
Die castingNet ÷ casting yield %Gross × (1 + melt loss %)
FoundryNet ÷ casting yield %Gross × (1 + melt loss %)
Sheet metal(Blank L + gap) × (blank W + gap) × thickness × density= gross
Net RM costMaterial used × rate/kg − (gross − net) × scrap value/kg

These formulas are not written in the program. They are rows in Master data → Countries, machines & formulas (scopes rm_plastic, rm_die_casting, rm_foundry, rm_sheet_metal), so you can change them. The material rate and scrap value are approved rates of the producing country (Rates page, categories material and material_scrap) and exist for all nine countries. Every number on the page is clickable.

Material & process rates by country

Rates → Material & process rates shows part materials, tool steels (P20, H13, 2738, D2, SKD11, mould base steel …) and process rates (heat treatment per kg, wire cut per cm², polishing per cm², painting per m², transport per kg …) side by side for every country, in the country's currency or INR, with the difference against India. They are normal approved, versioned rates. The list and the India values are in Master data → Mould & die database; the price level of each country against India is Master data → Countries, machines & formulas → Country rate index. Propose missing rates saves drafts (India × index ÷ forex) only where a country has no rate yet. Seeded values are EXAMPLES (•): replace them with quotes or market rates.

RM cost of a registered part

The Parts page shows the RM cost of every part that is made by you (moulding, casting, sheet metal) and has a material. It is stored as a numbered version with its full trace and the rate versions it used, and a new version is made when the 3D file, material, process, country, month, inserts or your typed inputs change. A newly approved rate does not change a stored cost: the part shows “Newer approved rates exist” and you click Recalculate with latest rates (or Recalculate RM on the Dashboard for the whole project). Use Open in Estimate on a part to work on it with the full Stage B–C page and Save to part to keep your changes.

Stage C

Cavities & number of tools

StepFormula
Planned hours / yearWorking days × shifts × hours per shift
Productive hours / yearPlanned hours × OEE (OEE is applied once, here)
Cavities neededAnnual volume ÷ productive hours × cycle time (s) ÷ 3600
Cavities per toolNeeded, rounded up to an allowed count; no more than fit on the plate or the maximum
Tools in parallelceil(cavities needed ÷ cavities per tool)
Tools over programmemax(tools in parallel, ceil(shots over programme ÷ tool life))
Machine utilisation(shots × cycle ÷ 3600 ÷ OEE) ÷ (planned hours × tools in parallel)

Plastic cycle time is estimated from the main wall: base time + cooling factor × wall². Replace it with your own figure.

Rates

Machine hour rates, labour rates & approvals

ValueFormula (stored in Master data → Countries, machines & formulas)
Machine hours / yearWorking days × shifts × hours per shift × utilisation %
Depreciation(Price − residual) ÷ life years ÷ machine hours
Interest(Price + residual) ÷ 2 × interest % ÷ machine hours
PowerkW × load factor × electricity rate
Maintenance · floor space · consumablesPrice × maintenance % ÷ hours · m² × rent × 12 ÷ hours · rate per hour
MHRMachine cost subtotal × (1 + overhead %)
Labour rateSalary × 12 × (1 + employer %) ÷ (days × hours per shift) × (1 + supervision %) ÷ productive %

Every rate has monthly versions. Only approved versions are used, and approved versions are never changed: a new value is a new version (Draft, or Save & approve). Click any number on the Rates page to see its formula, inputs, sources, approval and history.

Tool cost

Mould / die size & tool cost

StepRule (all from master data and the rate library)
Insert per cavityPart L × W + 2 × insert wall; height per half = part H ÷ 2 + backing
Cavity layoutEvery rows × columns grid is tried; the one needing the smallest standard base wins
Mould baseSmallest base whose insert pocket takes all inserts (either way round)
SteelQuantity × weight (BOM rule) × approved steel rate of the tool country
Bought-outQuantity × catalogue price (BOM rule, INR) converted to the tool country's currency
Machine hoursHours (BOM rule with Process time parameters) × (MHR of the machine + operators × labour rate) — Tool processes names the machine, labour grade and operators
Per-unit processesAmount (kg, cm², …) × rate by unit, at least the minimum charge per job (cutting, heat treatment, nitriding, texture, …)
Try-outClamp force picks the smallest moulding / die-casting machine; trials × hours × (MHR + crew) + trial shots × material rate
Tool cost per toolManufacturing cost + overhead % + profit % + packing % + transport per kg + warranty / spares % (formulas of scope tool)
Tool CAPEXTool cost × tools over the programme (Stage C)

Click any value for its formula and sources: an hour opens to the MHR, which opens to depreciation, power, labour and the rest. The red Uses example rates – not for negotiation warning is on every tool cost (screen, Excel, PDF) while any rate or master-data row it uses is still an EXAMPLE; it says how many of each are left. On the Parts page each part keeps numbered tool cost versions (tool country, cavities and features per part); newer rates never change a stored version — use Recalculate, or Recalculate tools on the Dashboard, which also shows the tool CAPEX against the budget.

Master data

Keeping rates and rules up to date

General master data · Excel

master_data/master_data.xlsx has three sheets: Parameters (defaults and rules), Materials (density; the RM rate and scrap value are approved rates on the Rates page) and Machines (tonnage, tie-bar size, MHR).

Mould & die database · SQLite

master_data/costing_db.sqlite has mould bases, tool steels, BOM elements with rules, process rates, sizing rules, tool processes (machine, labour grade, operators per process) and process time parameters. Export it to Excel, edit it, and import it back.

Saving is checked

A wrong value is refused with its sheet and row number, the row is highlighted, and the file stays unchanged.

Writing rules

One-line formulas such as max(4, ceil(cavities * projected_area_cm2 / 8)) or cavities if hot_runner else 0. The list of variables is on the Master data page.

Simulation, correction, tool design

From the simulation result to released tool drawings

Simulation report and video

Sheet metal: Design loop → Forming simulation → ⬇ PDF (every check OK / FAIL, blank and yield, stages, force, press, a picture after every stage, flat pattern with bend lines) and ▶ Video (blank, piercing, bending; a failing bend is red). Plastic / die casting: Tool cost → Simulation & analysis → PDF and Video of the wall, draft and risk maps. Analytical results from the rules in Master data, not a finite-element simulation.

Correct the 3D model

When the forming check fails on the bend radius or a hole size: Correct the 3D model. The software sets the new inside radius (outside size kept) or opens the holes, shows before / after with the bend profile, gives the corrected STEP and releases it as a new revision (old revisions stay, costs are recalculated). Other changes (draft, walls, ribs): in CAD, then Upload a new revision.

Tool design in 3D

Tool cost → Tool design → Make the tool design: each die stage (blanking, piercing, bending, progressive) or the mould (plastic, die casting, EPS, blow) is designed in 3D from the part, the flat pattern and the tool cost: shoes or plates, die plate / inserts with the real opening or core and cavity, punches with clearance, stripper, guides, pillars, bushes, ejection, cooling, screws, dowels. Sizes, fits, tolerances and finishes are Design rules in Master data → Mould & die database.

Drawings and release pack

Drawing set (PDF): assembly sheet (iso, exploded, section, BOM) and a detail sheet per made part (views, dimensions, hole table with fits, tolerances, material, hardness, heat treatment, finish). Release pack (ZIP): assembly STEP, a STEP per part, PDF and DXF of every drawing, BOM Excel. Your logo, company and signatures: Admin → Drawings. A first-cut design by rules for your tool designer to check and release.

Admin & suppliers

Logins, admin control and suppliers

Logins

Everyone logs in with their own ID. The very first start asks for the admin ID and password. A forgotten password is reset at the PC that runs the app with DTC_ResetPassword.bat.

Admin page

Users & roles: create IDs, roles, block users. Permissions: the lowest role for each action. Activity log: who changed what and when, with Excel export. Backups & update: backup now, download, version.

Supplier master

Codes come automatically from supplier_code_pattern (SUP-0001…). Each supplier has status, capability, contacts, certifications and quality / delivery / cost scores; their average is the rating.

Quotes vs should-cost

On a part, add its main and alternate suppliers with their piece and tool price. The quote is converted to INR with the approved forex and compared with our part cost and tool cost. CAPEX lines take the supplier by its code.

Dies and tool BOM

Sheet-metal parts get one die per operation (or one progressive die), each with its BOM and cost. On every tool BOM, change grade, make, standard part (with part number) or price; add new grades and standard parts; the software learns your choices for the next parts.

Correct any value

Click a value, then in the traceability window change it or its formula for this part only, or save for the future (rate library or master data). Mould base / die set: pick another or add one.

Table filters

Every table has a ▾ in each column header: search, tick several values, Select all, Clear all.
